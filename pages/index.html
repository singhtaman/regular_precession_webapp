About this figure and model

Stellar binary black holes coalescing in the LIGO band can have misaligned spins. The orbital angular momentum L then precesses and nutates about the total angular momentum J, modulating the gravitational-wave signal. In regular precession L moves on a cone around J with no nutation, and the direction of J is nearly constant over the radiation-reaction time.

Two dimensionless parameters describe the cone in the post-Newtonian waveform: the precession amplitude θ̃ sets its opening angle, θLJ = 0.1 θ̃ (f/fcut)1/3, and the precession frequency Ω̃ sets how fast L moves around it. The precessional phase γP is a nuisance parameter. The panels compare a regularly precessing signal (RP, blue) with the non-precessing signal (NP) of the same source.

  • Modulation of L: the direction of L in the detector frame, cos θL against φL. The red point marks the direction of J.
  • Amplitude: fractional change of the GW amplitude, BRP/BNP − 1.
  • Phase: phase difference ΦRP − ΦNP in radians: the precessional polarization phase plus twice the precession correction δφ, with the sign used by the original app.

Time domain: the strain panel shows h(t), the inverse Fourier transform of the frequency-domain detector strain h̃(f) = B(f) ei(Ψ − φP − 2δφ) on the same frequency grid, for RP and NP. Ψ is the 2PN orbital phase with tc = φc = 0, so t = 0 is the coalescence time; the signal reaches the cut-off frequency a few milliseconds earlier (4 ms at the default parameters). The band edges are tapered with a Tukey window (α = 0.1) so the sharp start at 20 Hz and cut-off at fcut don't ring. The modulation of L, the sphere and the system properties are the same in both views.

Sphere (both views): directions on the unit sphere in the source frame with J at the north pole and the x axis from which the model measures φLJ. L = (sin θLJ cos φLJ, sin θLJ sin φLJ, cos θLJ); the blue band spans the polar angles θLJ takes from 20 Hz to fcut, and the blue curve is the path of L. The orbital plane is perpendicular to L, so its great circle is tilted by θLJ and sweeps the band of latitudes within ±max θLJ of the equator (orange). The line of sight is N̂ = (0, sin ιJN, cos ιJN), the direction for which the model's L̂·N̂ = sin θLJ sin ιJN sin φLJ + cos θLJ cos ιJN holds. A time on the strain plot, or a frequency on the amplitude and phase plots, is matched to L by the stationary-phase relation t(f) = (1/2π) dΨ/df of the orbital phase Ψ (tc = 0), with the derivative taken numerically on the frequency grid; times outside t(20 Hz) to t(fcut) have no L marker. Play runs from 20 Hz to fcut at the chosen fraction of real time: L leaves a trail on the sphere, and the strain after the current time (time view) or the amplitude and phase above the current frequency (frequency view) are faded behind a cursor; the strain view scrolls along when zoomed in. The time slider and a click on a linked plot pause it at a chosen time.

Frequencies run from 20 Hz up to the cut-off fcut = 1/(63/2 π Mz) in steps of 0.05 Hz. Distances use flat ΛCDM with Planck 2018 parameters (H0 = 67.4, Ωm = 0.315). Plots open on the original app's axis windows and extend them when the curves would otherwise run outside.

Drag a slider or type a value; plots update when you release the handle. Use the plot toolbar to zoom, pan or save a PNG, and double-click a plot to reset its view.
